## 2. Procedures for HVAC System Calibration
Accuracy in climate control is dependent on the precise synchronization of hardware response with environmental setpoints. The following calibration steps must be executed bi-annually or after any component replacement:

*   **Zero-Point Calibration:** Ensure all actuators and dampers are set to the absolute zero position within the digital control interface.
*   **Voltage Verification:** Measure the signal output from the central controller to the variable air volume (VAV) boxes to ensure linear response across the 0-10V range.
*   **Discharge Air Temperature (DAT) Alignment:** Adjust the heating and cooling coil valves to ensure the delivered air temperature matches the sensor feedback within a tolerance of ±0.5°F.

## 3. Peripheral Monitoring Sensor Placement Guidelines
To optimize environmental tracking, peripheral sensors must be positioned to reflect the true ambient conditions of the zone. When moving sensors, follow these guidelines:

*   **Height Requirements:** Sensors must be mounted between 48 and 60 inches from the finished floor.
*   **Thermal Interference:** Avoid mounting sensors on exterior walls or within 3 feet of supply air diffusers and heat-generating equipment.
*   **Relocation Methodology:** When moving a sensor to optimize tracking, the central monitoring station must be placed in "Bypass Mode" to prevent false alarms during the transition. Re-verify the communication link immediately following the physical relocation.

## 4. Standard Checklists for Equipment Installation
Before a system is integrated into the primary network, the following installation checklist must be completed and filed:

*   [ ] Mechanical mounting of unit is secure and vibration-isolated.
*   [ ] Electrical connections verified against wiring diagrams; grounding confirmed.
*   [ ] Condensate drainage lines checked for proper slope and trap depth.
*   [ ] Filter rack integrity confirmed; no air bypass present.
*   [ ] Control wiring terminated and labeled according to the master schematic.
*   [ ] Initial power-up sequence observed without fault codes.

## 5. Methodologies for Thermal Stability Monitoring
Consistent environmental tracking requires rigorous monitoring methodologies to ensure the hardware maintains thermal stability over time:

*   **Differential Analysis:** Compare readings between the return air sensors and peripheral zone sensors to identify stratification issues.
*   **Control Loop Tuning:** Utilize Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) tuning to reduce temperature oscillation around the setpoint. Ensure the "I" (Integral) component is adjusted to eliminate steady-state error without causing system hunting.
