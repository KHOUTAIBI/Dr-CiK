3. OBSERVED TRAFFIC CHARACTERISTICS

3.1 Density Patterns
Vista-Point Node 4 on Coastal Linkway Route 9 typically records a high-density traffic pattern. Unlike auxiliary routes within the network, the throughput at this specific node is characterized by a high volume of vehicles per lane-mile. This density is a persistent feature of the node’s primary operational environment.

3.2 Off-Peak Utilization
Analysis of sensor data indicates that occupancy levels at Node 4 maintain a substantial baseline even during traditional off-peak hours. While typical network nodes experience sharp declines in volume following evening peak hours, Node 4 demonstrates a resilient level of occupancy that deviates from standard municipal traffic models.

3.3 Nocturnal Traffic Flow
Traffic usage at Node 4 remains consistently elevated throughout the night. The data suggests that nocturnal activity levels do not follow the standard dormancy curves found elsewhere on Route 9. Instead, the node supports a steady stream of traffic that persists from the late evening into the early morning hours without significant interruption.

4. SENSOR DATA INTEGRITY
Occupancy values at this sensor avoid the significant drops toward zero commonly seen in lower-utilization sensors. Whereas other sensors in the Traffic Management Division’s network may periodically report values approaching a true zero during quiet periods, Node 4 maintains a continuous positive occupancy value. This indicates a constant presence of vehicles within the detection zone, reflecting the node's unique position and importance within the Coastal Linkway infrastructure.

5. CONCLUSION
Node 4 functions as a high-utilization link within the Route 9 corridor. Its characteristics—marked by high density, high off-peak baselines, and consistent nighttime usage—ensure that occupancy data remains at a high threshold across all 24-hour cycles. This profile serves as the definitive baseline for monitoring the technical health and operational expectations of the Vista-Point Node 4 hardware.
